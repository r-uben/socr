# 2026-09-12 — #735 / #734: the Stage 1 reader on the Fed SEP dot-plot pages

Branch `fix/735-sep-reader-fabrication` off `main@3cbf8a9`.

## What was wrong

On 10 of the 23 Fed SEP projection pages the Stage 1 chart reader published a table it
had invented: the footnote's words as column headers (`Definitions | of | variables | …`)
with hard zeros beneath them, under the banner that says the counts were read from the
source. The other 13 pages published nothing at all. Five separate defects stacked up to
that, and each is fixed here.

1. **An invisible wrapper rectangle counted as ink** (#734). Each SEP panel is wrapped in
   a white-on-white `fs` rectangle 445.5 × 114.6 pt. Region clustering saw five boxes half
   a point apart and merged all five panels into ONE region. Every page in the corpus
   came back with a single region.
2. **The tick ladders at the two ends of one axis were compared for exact equality.** The
   Fed's generator rounds the left and right copies of the same ladder apart by 0.036 to
   0.053 pt, against a 0.336 pt tick stroke. `find_frame` called that a contradiction and
   refused the axis, so the Dec 2020 → Dec 2023 pages found no frame at all.
3. **The bins were "the densest row of text below the axis"** — a popularity contest the
   page's footnote wins. That is the fabrication itself.
4. **The calibration gated measured heights but not zeros.** `_resolve` already refuses a
   height whose uncertainty is not below half a count, but an empty bin is read as zero
   from the ABSENCE of a mark and never passes through it. On the merged region the y
   scale fitted at a residual of 211–230 pt against a ~2.1 pt half-count and still emitted
   a full row of confident zeros.
5. **The prior-meeting staircase is one compound path.** Nine path items under a single
   bounding rectangle that is neither horizontal nor vertical. The reader dropped the
   piece and read on; with no run and no riser left, every bin fell to the axis and
   published a hard zero for a series the page visibly draws.

## What the fixes are

Each is threshold-free — it reads the page's own declared geometry, and no new constant
was added.

- `tables/reconstruct.py::_paints_nothing` — a drawing paints only through the channels
  its operator declares, and a channel shows nothing when it is absent, its opacity is
  zero, or its colour is the page ground. Such a drawing is excluded from
  `_drawing_bboxes`, so it can neither anchor a cluster nor bridge two.
- `chart_reader.py::_ladders_agree` — two ladders agree when they tick the same number of
  heights and every pair is within the widest half stroke width the marks were drawn at.
  Half a stroke width is the finest an edge can be located at, so it is the resolution the
  comparison is made at.
- `chart_reader.py::read_bins` — the label row is the FIRST row below the axis carrying
  more than one token, and every one of its tokens must be drawn inside the axis' own
  horizontal span. Both conditions are geometric and frame-attached; neither reads what
  the tokens say, because the bins of a bar chart need not be numeric. Failing either
  leaves the panel with no bins and `read_chart_page` refuses it.
- `chart_reader.py::read_chart_page` — a panel whose calibration residual is not below
  half a count publishes nothing at all, including the zeros read off absence.
- `chart_reader.py::read_legend` — a DATA mark spans at least a whole bin AND covers that
  bin's printed label centre. Either half alone rejects real evidence: the SEP legends sit
  inside the plot directly above a bin label, so requiring only the second discarded them.
  Naming asks that the swatch's centre lie within the naming row's own painted extent, and
  that the nearest token to the swatch be inside the plot's span — the same discriminator
  `calibrate_y` uses in reverse, which is what stops a y tick label at the far edge from
  joining the series name.
- `chart_reader.py::read_dashed_series` — a piece of the outline that is neither a
  horizontal run nor a vertical riser means the outline was not reconstructed. The series
  is PRESENT with every cell UNRESOLVED and a detail naming the offending path, instead of
  a column of zeros.

`READER_VERSION` moves `635-stage1/1` → `635-stage1/2`, since published counts move.

### Why `tables/reconstruct.py` and not `figures/chart_regions.py`

The brief named `chart_regions.py` for the invisible-rect fix. There is no such module on
this branch. `chart_region_bboxes`, `_drawing_bboxes` and `_union_find_clusters` — the
region clustering the SEP pages collapse in — live in `src/socr/tables/reconstruct.py`,
and `chart_reader.py` imports `chart_region_bboxes` from there. The fix is in the function
the brief describes; only the file name differs.

## Honest residuals

- **The prior-meeting series is UNRESOLVED on every SEP panel.** That is the compound-path
  guard doing its job: the staircase is emitted as one path and this reader cannot
  decompose it. It is a recall loss, not a correctness loss — every one of those 1 164
  cells carries a detail saying exactly why. Decomposing the path items is follow-up work.
  On a few panels (e.g. `sep-20210922` r4) the series' PRESENCE is itself unresolved.
- **Nine panels are refused for legend grouping**, all with "no legend on this figure binds
  a series name to a drawing style": `sep-20220921` r2–r5, `sep-20221214` r2–r5, and
  `sep-20240320` r3. The figure-level legend grouping keys on (bin labels, tick values),
  and these panels print a different bin count or tick set from the legend-bearing panel,
  so they land in a different group and inherit no legend. Not a fabrication — a refusal —
  but it costs 9 of 108 panels.
- **Bin labels carry the printed minus sign**, e.g. `2.13−-2.37`, because the SEP pages set
  the range across two lines with a trailing U+2212 on the first. Cosmetic; the label is
  what the page drew.
- The reference `dotplot-p20.pdf` is **byte-identical**: 5 panels, the same counts, no
  refusals, checked before and after every change in this branch.
