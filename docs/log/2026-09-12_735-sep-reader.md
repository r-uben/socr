# 2026-09-12 — #735 / #734: the Stage 1 reader on the Fed SEP dot-plot pages

Branch `fix/735-sep-reader-fabrication` off `main@3cbf8a9`.

## What was wrong

On 10 of the 23 Fed SEP projection pages the Stage 1 chart reader published a table it
had invented: the footnote's words as column headers (`Definitions | of | variables | …`)
with hard zeros beneath them, under the banner that says the counts were read from the
source. The other 13 pages published nothing at all. Five separate defects stacked up to
that, and each is fixed here.

1. **An invisible wrapper rectangle counted as ink** (#734). Each SEP panel is wrapped in
   a white-on-white `fs` rectangle 445.5 × 114.6 pt. Region clustering saw five boxes half
   a point apart and merged all five panels into ONE region. Every page in the corpus
   came back with a single region.
2. **The tick ladders at the two ends of one axis were compared for exact equality.** The
   Fed's generator rounds the left and right copies of the same ladder apart by 0.036 to
   0.053 pt, against a 0.336 pt tick stroke. `find_frame` called that a contradiction and
   refused the axis, so the Dec 2020 → Dec 2023 pages found no frame at all.
3. **The bins were "the densest row of text below the axis"** — a popularity contest the
   page's footnote wins. That is the fabrication itself.
4. **The calibration gated measured heights but not zeros.** `_resolve` already refuses a
   height whose uncertainty is not below half a count, but an empty bin is read as zero
   from the ABSENCE of a mark and never passes through it. On the merged region the y
   scale fitted at a residual of 211–230 pt against a ~2.1 pt half-count and still emitted
   a full row of confident zeros.
5. **The prior-meeting staircase is one compound path.** Nine path items under a single
   bounding rectangle that is neither horizontal nor vertical. The reader dropped the
   piece and read on; with no run and no riser left, every bin fell to the axis and
   published a hard zero for a series the page visibly draws.

## What the fixes are

Each is threshold-free — it reads the page's own declared geometry, and no new constant
was added.

- `tables/reconstruct.py::_paints_nothing` — a drawing paints only through the channels
  its operator declares, and a channel shows nothing when it is absent, its opacity is
  zero, or its colour is the page ground. Such a drawing is excluded from
  `_drawing_bboxes`, so it can neither anchor a cluster nor bridge two.
- `chart_reader.py::_ladders_agree` — two ladders agree when they tick the same number of
  heights and every pair is within the widest half stroke width the marks were drawn at.
  Half a stroke width is the finest an edge can be located at, so it is the resolution the
  comparison is made at.
- `chart_reader.py::read_bins` — the label row is the FIRST row below the axis carrying
  more than one token, and every one of its tokens must be drawn inside the axis' own
  horizontal span. Both conditions are geometric and frame-attached; neither reads what
  the tokens say, because the bins of a bar chart need not be numeric. Failing either
  leaves the panel with no bins and `read_chart_page` refuses it.
- `chart_reader.py::read_chart_page` — a panel whose calibration residual is not below
  half a count publishes nothing at all, including the zeros read off absence.
- `chart_reader.py::read_legend` — a DATA mark spans at least a whole bin AND covers that
  bin's printed label centre. Either half alone rejects real evidence: the SEP legends sit
  inside the plot directly above a bin label, so requiring only the second discarded them.
  Naming asks that the swatch's centre lie within the naming row's own painted extent, and
  that the nearest token to the swatch be inside the plot's span — the same discriminator
  `calibrate_y` uses in reverse, which is what stops a y tick label at the far edge from
  joining the series name.
- `chart_reader.py::read_dashed_series` — a piece of the outline that is neither a
  horizontal run nor a vertical riser means the outline was not reconstructed. The series
  is PRESENT with every cell UNRESOLVED and a detail naming the offending path, instead of
  a column of zeros.

`READER_VERSION` moves `635-stage1/1` → `635-stage1/2`, since published counts move.

### Why `tables/reconstruct.py` and not `figures/chart_regions.py`

The brief named `chart_regions.py` for the invisible-rect fix. There is no such module on
this branch. `chart_region_bboxes`, `_drawing_bboxes` and `_union_find_clusters` — the
region clustering the SEP pages collapse in — live in `src/socr/tables/reconstruct.py`,
and `chart_reader.py` imports `chart_region_bboxes` from there. The fix is in the function
the brief describes; only the file name differs.

## Honest residuals

- **The prior-meeting series is UNRESOLVED on every SEP panel.** That is the compound-path
  guard doing its job: the staircase is emitted as one path and this reader cannot
  decompose it. It is a recall loss, not a correctness loss — every one of those 1 164
  cells carries a detail saying exactly why. Decomposing the path items is follow-up work.
  On a few panels (e.g. `sep-20210922` r4) the series' PRESENCE is itself unresolved.
- **Nine panels are refused for legend grouping**, all with "no legend on this figure binds
  a series name to a drawing style": `sep-20220921` r2–r5, `sep-20221214` r2–r5, and
  `sep-20240320` r3. The figure-level legend grouping keys on (bin labels, tick values),
  and these panels print a different bin count or tick set from the legend-bearing panel,
  so they land in a different group and inherit no legend. Not a fabrication — a refusal —
  but it costs 9 of 108 panels.
- **Bin labels carry the printed minus sign**, e.g. `2.13−-2.37`, because the SEP pages set
  the range across two lines with a trailing U+2212 on the first. Cosmetic; the label is
  what the page drew.
- The reference `dotplot-p20.pdf` is **count-identical**: 5 panels, the same 65 cells, the
  same counts, no refusals, checked before and after every change in this branch. Not
  byte-identical — `READER_VERSION` is serialised into the derivation's metadata and its
  banner, so those bytes move with every bump on this branch.

## Round 2 — the reviewer's findings

The round-1 branch was reviewed at `4f56a5e`. It reproduced every number in the table
above independently, read a further 175 FOMC minutes pages (750 regions, 635 panels, 9 971
integers, 0 published cells without a tick-attached bin), and found one P1 and two P2s.

### P1 — the bin row rule was still a text-position rule, and it regressed

"The first row below the axis, all tokens inside the span" is a proximity contest, and the
chart's own x-unit annotation wins it. A two-word `Percent range` set between the axis and
its labels became the bins; the printed labels were then absorbed into it as a second atom
line by `_aligned`; three of four bars vanished and the survivor was published under a
column the page never labelled. `main@3cbf8a9` read that page correctly, so this was a
regression introduced by round 1, not a surviving gap.

Density and adjacency are both properties of where a generator happens to put its text.
The property actually being asserted is that **the row the marks are assigned to is a row
the marks corroborate**, so that is what `read_bins` now asks. A candidate row carries more
than one token, all inside the axis' span. Where more than one row qualifies, the bars
choose: a row's corroboration is the number of bars standing on this axis that cover
exactly one of its token centres, which is `_owned_bins` used as an admission test. The
best-corroborated row wins, ties going to the upper one, because a label printed over two
lines puts both lines in the running. A best corroboration of zero means the drawing picks
no row, and the panel is refused.

Where only ONE row qualifies the bars are not asked, because there is nothing for them to
choose between. That keeps the existing behaviour for a panel whose single label row is
drawn against unplaceable bars: the panel still reads, and those bars still make their own
bins UNRESOLVED through `_doubted_by`. Corroboration decides which row, never whether a bar
is good — the strict form of the rule refused fourteen of the #635 suite's own panels.

### P2 — `_paints_nothing` tested a white literal, not the page ground

The docstring claimed a page-ground comparison; the code asked whether every channel was at
least 1.0. On a page painted dark that is wrong in both directions: a wrapper in the page's
own colour is invisible and was kept, and a genuinely visible white shape was discarded.
`_page_ground` now derives the ground from the page's own drawings — the last opaque fill
covering the whole page, defaulting to white — and `_same_colour` compares through `_to_rgb`,
so grey, RGB and CMYK declarations of one colour compare equal. Measured through
`chart_region_bboxes`, all four combinations now behave: on white, a white wrapper gives 2
regions and a grey one 1; on a dark ground, the dark wrapper gives 2 and a white one 1.

### P2 — the derived bin label was not a well-formed Stage 0 key

The page sets a two-line range with the connector at the end of its upper line, as U+2212
on the Fed pages and as an ASCII hyphen elsewhere. Joining the atoms as drawn produced
`2.13--2.37`, which Stage 0's `_key_atoms` rejects as a key with an empty part: 2 072 of
7 077 labels across 46 of 198 pages, so a derived grid could never be matched to a withheld
one. `_join_atoms` strips a trailing range dash from any atom that has another atom after
it, and nowhere else. All 1 217 SEP bin labels now parse; the reference's labels are
unchanged.

### The blast radius outside dot-plots

`_drawing_bboxes` feeds `chart_region_bboxes`, which feeds the table rowizer's chart-clip
and the #189 crops on every document, not only dot-plots — so this branch changes which
words a table is allowed to keep. The reviewer compared region boxes against `main` over the
census and ladder fixture corpora and found 10 of 84 pages changed, with both spot checks
improvements: a BoE scan's full-page box shrinks to the band that actually carries ink, and
`doc04` page 4, whose only drawing is a white rectangle, stops manufacturing a full-page
chart region on a page with no chart. I re-ran that census on the 77 pages still present
(two fixture PDFs have since been removed) and the page-ground change of round 2 moves
**none** of them: the derived ground is white on every one, so the behaviour is identical to
round 1 there.

### Corpora unchanged

The 23 SEP pages and the reference read exactly as tabulated above after all three round-2

### Two residuals recorded rather than charged

Both are in `docs/plans/chart-data/STATUS.md` as items 12 and 13.

- **A region holding two panels reads one and says nothing about the other.** If only the
  lower plot carries a tick ladder, the calibration closes cleanly off it, the lower panel
  reads correctly, and the upper is neither read nor refused. The residual gate catches this
  only when the two ladders collide. This is the one-region-one-panel assumption, older than
  this branch, and it is the last silent loss left in the reader.
- **`_ladders_agree` has no bound relative to the tick pitch.** A 4 pt disagreement against a
  20 pt pitch is refused at a 0.4 pt stroke and admitted at a 9 pt one. It cannot produce a
  wrong number: a displaced ladder moves the fitted zero, which lands in the residual, which
  both the panel gate and `_resolve` charge.

## Round 2 addendum — the second reviewer

A second independent review concurred with both findings above (its own counterexample is a
`Definitions of variables` caption drawn between the axis and the labels, which published
`[0, 0, 0]` at a residual of exactly 0.0) and added two more.

### Two plot frames in one region, with no invisible wrapper involved

Round 1 stopped the SEP regions merging by dropping the wrapper rectangles that paint
nothing. That fixes the observed corpus and leaves the failure reachable: two charts drawn
on ONE light-grey background are bridged by the real `chart_region_bboxes`, because the grey
is genuine ink. `find_frame` then picked the lower axis, calibrated against its ladder at a
residual of 0.0, `_panel_label` took the UPPER title, and the region published
`FIRST PANEL / June projections = [7, 0, 0, 0]` — the lower chart's bar, under the upper
chart's name, with no refusal anywhere. A perfect residual says one ladder fits one scale.
It says nothing about which of two plots the ink belongs to.

`find_frames` now returns every frame in a set of marks rather than only the lowest. The
candidate axes are grouped by the LADDER each reads, and each group yields one frame: a
plot's own top rule encloses the same ticks as its axis, so a boxed chart is one frame, not
two (pinned by `test_one_plot_drawn_with_a_top_rule_is_still_one_frame` — a rule that counted
candidate axes would have refused every boxed chart in the corpus). `read_chart_page` refuses
a region with more than one frame, naming the baselines.

Refusal rather than a split, deliberately: the region index is the identity the crops, the
Stage 0 notes and the suppression events are all keyed on, so the reader cannot invent new
region indices without breaking that binding. Separating two bridged charts is the region
detector's job. The surviving hole — an upper plot with no tick ladder of its own, which
leaves one frame to find and so no competition to detect — is recorded as STATUS item 12.

### White is ink unless the page's own ground says otherwise

The reviewer's ruling on `_paints_nothing` was stricter than "fix the docstring": an opaque
white object on a non-white background IS ink, and one on a white page can erase what was
drawn under it. The round-2 fix already derives the page ground from the page's own drawings
(`_page_ground`: the last opaque fill covering the whole page rect, white when there is
none) and treats a channel as invisible only when it is absent, zero-opacity, or the ground
colour. On a dark page a white rectangle is therefore ink; on a white page a white wrapper
is not. The reviewer's alternative — never exclude on colour at all — would put the SEP
wrappers back and re-merge the five panels, which the competing-frames refusal would then
turn into five refused pages instead of five read ones. Deriving the ground keeps both.

What remains, and is not fixed: a white rectangle drawn OVER earlier content on a white page
erases it, and is still excluded here. That costs a region the reader might otherwise have
been told about; it cannot publish a number.

### #734 is not closed

`_derive_chart_tables` (`src/socr/pipeline/orchestrator.py:3508`) returns before any chart
derivation when `find_empty_skeletons(text)` is false, so a model grid that is FILLED with
invented numbers never reaches the reader and nothing reconciles it against the drawing.
This branch fixes the reader-side half of #734 — five panels no longer collapse into one
region — and not the reconciliation half. The PR must not say `Closes #734`. Recorded as
STATUS item 14.
