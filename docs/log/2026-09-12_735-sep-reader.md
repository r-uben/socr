# 2026-09-12 — #735 / #734: the Stage 1 reader on the Fed SEP dot-plot pages

Branch `fix/735-sep-reader-fabrication` off `main@3cbf8a9`.

## What was wrong

On 10 of the 23 Fed SEP projection pages the Stage 1 chart reader published a table it
had invented: the footnote's words as column headers (`Definitions | of | variables | …`)
with hard zeros beneath them, under the banner that says the counts were read from the
source. The other 13 pages published nothing at all. Five separate defects stacked up to
that, and each is fixed here.

1. **An invisible wrapper rectangle counted as ink** (#734). Each SEP panel is wrapped in
   a white-on-white `fs` rectangle 445.5 × 114.6 pt. Region clustering saw five boxes half
   a point apart and merged all five panels into ONE region. Every page in the corpus
   came back with a single region.
2. **The tick ladders at the two ends of one axis were compared for exact equality.** The
   Fed's generator rounds the left and right copies of the same ladder apart by 0.036 to
   0.053 pt, against a 0.336 pt tick stroke. `find_frame` called that a contradiction and
   refused the axis, so the Dec 2020 → Dec 2023 pages found no frame at all.
3. **The bins were "the densest row of text below the axis"** — a popularity contest the
   page's footnote wins. That is the fabrication itself.
4. **The calibration gated measured heights but not zeros.** `_resolve` already refuses a
   height whose uncertainty is not below half a count, but an empty bin is read as zero
   from the ABSENCE of a mark and never passes through it. On the merged region the y
   scale fitted at a residual of 211–230 pt against a ~2.1 pt half-count and still emitted
   a full row of confident zeros.
5. **The prior-meeting staircase is one compound path.** Nine path items under a single
   bounding rectangle that is neither horizontal nor vertical. The reader dropped the
   piece and read on; with no run and no riser left, every bin fell to the axis and
   published a hard zero for a series the page visibly draws.

## What the fixes are

Each is threshold-free — it reads the page's own declared geometry, and no new constant
was added.

- `tables/reconstruct.py::_paints_nothing` — a drawing paints only through the channels
  its operator declares, and a channel shows nothing when it is absent, its opacity is
  zero, or its colour is the page ground. Such a drawing is excluded from
  `_drawing_bboxes`, so it can neither anchor a cluster nor bridge two.
- `chart_reader.py::_ladders_agree` — two ladders agree when they tick the same number of
  heights and every pair is within the widest half stroke width the marks were drawn at.
  Half a stroke width is the finest an edge can be located at, so it is the resolution the
  comparison is made at.
- `chart_reader.py::read_bins` — the label row is the FIRST row below the axis carrying
  more than one token, and every one of its tokens must be drawn inside the axis' own
  horizontal span. Both conditions are geometric and frame-attached; neither reads what
  the tokens say, because the bins of a bar chart need not be numeric. Failing either
  leaves the panel with no bins and `read_chart_page` refuses it.
- `chart_reader.py::read_chart_page` — a panel whose calibration residual is not below
  half a count publishes nothing at all, including the zeros read off absence.
- `chart_reader.py::read_legend` — a DATA mark spans at least a whole bin AND covers that
  bin's printed label centre. Either half alone rejects real evidence: the SEP legends sit
  inside the plot directly above a bin label, so requiring only the second discarded them.
  Naming asks that the swatch's centre lie within the naming row's own painted extent, and
  that the nearest token to the swatch be inside the plot's span — the same discriminator
  `calibrate_y` uses in reverse, which is what stops a y tick label at the far edge from
  joining the series name.
- `chart_reader.py::read_dashed_series` — a piece of the outline that is neither a
  horizontal run nor a vertical riser means the outline was not reconstructed. The series
  is PRESENT with every cell UNRESOLVED and a detail naming the offending path, instead of
  a column of zeros.

`READER_VERSION` moves `635-stage1/1` → `635-stage1/2`, since published counts move.

### Why `tables/reconstruct.py` and not `figures/chart_regions.py`

The brief named `chart_regions.py` for the invisible-rect fix. There is no such module on
this branch. `chart_region_bboxes`, `_drawing_bboxes` and `_union_find_clusters` — the
region clustering the SEP pages collapse in — live in `src/socr/tables/reconstruct.py`,
and `chart_reader.py` imports `chart_region_bboxes` from there. The fix is in the function
the brief describes; only the file name differs.

## Round 2 — the reviewer's findings

The round-1 branch was reviewed at `4f56a5e`. It reproduced every number in the table
above independently, read a further 175 FOMC minutes pages (750 regions, 635 panels, 9 971
integers, 0 published cells without a tick-attached bin), and found one P1 and two P2s.

### P1 — the bin row rule was still a text-position rule, and it regressed

"The first row below the axis, all tokens inside the span" is a proximity contest, and the
chart's own x-unit annotation wins it. A two-word `Percent range` set between the axis and
its labels became the bins; the printed labels were then absorbed into it as a second atom
line by `_aligned`; three of four bars vanished and the survivor was published under a
column the page never labelled. `main@3cbf8a9` read that page correctly, so this was a
regression introduced by round 1, not a surviving gap.

Density and adjacency are both properties of where a generator happens to put its text.
The property actually being asserted is that **the row the marks are assigned to is a row
the marks corroborate**, so that is what `read_bins` now asks. A candidate row carries more
than one token, all inside the axis' span. Where more than one row qualifies, the bars
choose: a row's corroboration is the number of bars standing on this axis that cover
exactly one of its token centres, which is `_owned_bins` used as an admission test. The
best-corroborated row wins, ties going to the upper one, because a label printed over two
lines puts both lines in the running. A best corroboration of zero means the drawing picks
no row, and the panel is refused.

Where only ONE row qualifies the bars are not asked, because there is nothing for them to
choose between. That keeps the existing behaviour for a panel whose single label row is
drawn against unplaceable bars: the panel still reads, and those bars still make their own
bins UNRESOLVED through `_doubted_by`. Corroboration decides which row, never whether a bar
is good — the strict form of the rule refused fourteen of the #635 suite's own panels.

### P2 — `_paints_nothing` tested a white literal, not the page ground

The docstring claimed a page-ground comparison; the code asked whether every channel was at
least 1.0. On a page painted dark that is wrong in both directions: a wrapper in the page's
own colour is invisible and was kept, and a genuinely visible white shape was discarded.
`_page_ground` now derives the ground from the page's own drawings — the last opaque fill
covering the whole page, defaulting to white — and `_same_colour` compares through `_to_rgb`,
so grey, RGB and CMYK declarations of one colour compare equal. Measured through
`chart_region_bboxes`, all four combinations now behave: on white, a white wrapper gives 2
regions and a grey one 1; on a dark ground, the dark wrapper gives 2 and a white one 1.

### P2 — the derived bin label was not a well-formed Stage 0 key

The page sets a two-line range with the connector at the end of its upper line, as U+2212
on the Fed pages and as an ASCII hyphen elsewhere. Joining the atoms as drawn produced
`2.13--2.37`, which Stage 0's `_key_atoms` rejects as a key with an empty part: 2 072 of
7 077 labels across 46 of 198 pages, so a derived grid could never be matched to a withheld
one. `_join_atoms` strips a trailing range dash from any atom that has another atom after
it, and nowhere else. All 1 217 SEP bin labels now parse; the reference's labels are
unchanged.

### The blast radius outside dot-plots

`_drawing_bboxes` feeds `chart_region_bboxes`, which feeds the table rowizer's chart-clip
and the #189 crops on every document, not only dot-plots — so this branch changes which
words a table is allowed to keep. My round-1 figure here (10 of 84 pages) was keyed on
basenames over an inconsistent root set; disregard it. The reviewer recomputed it from clean
`git archive` trees of `3cbf8a9`, `4f56a5e` and `816e18b` over one root list of 79 non-SEP
fixture pages with full-path keys:

- **main → round 1 changes 8 pages.** Every one is a tightening — the new box lies inside
  the old — and no page gains or loses a region.
- **Round 1 → round 2 changes 0 pages.** Across 277 pages of all six corpora, not one has a
  ground other than white, so the round-2 `_page_ground` rework is inert outside the
  synthetic tests.
- Two of the eight are large: `boe-speeches-2024-scan-p1-3.pdf` pages 1 and 2 go from the

## Round 2 addendum — the second reviewer

A second independent review concurred with both findings above (its own counterexample is a

### Two plot frames in one region, with no invisible wrapper involved

Round 1 stopped the SEP regions merging by dropping the wrapper rectangles that paint
nothing. That fixes the observed corpus and leaves the failure reachable: two charts drawn
on ONE light-grey background are bridged by the real `chart_region_bboxes`, because the grey
is genuine ink. `find_frame` then picked the lower axis, calibrated against its ladder at a
residual of 0.0, `_panel_label` took the UPPER title, and the region published
`FIRST PANEL / June projections = [7, 0, 0, 0]` — the lower chart's bar, under the upper
chart's name, with no refusal anywhere. A perfect residual says one ladder fits one scale.
It says nothing about which of two plots the ink belongs to.

`find_frames` now returns every frame in a set of marks rather than only the lowest. The
candidate axes are grouped by the LADDER each reads, and each group yields one frame: a
plot's own top rule encloses the same ticks as its axis, so a boxed chart is one frame, not
two (pinned by `test_one_plot_drawn_with_a_top_rule_is_still_one_frame` — a rule that counted
candidate axes would have refused every boxed chart in the corpus). `read_chart_page` refuses
a region with more than one frame, naming the baselines.

Refusal rather than a split, deliberately: the region index is the identity the crops, the
Stage 0 notes and the suppression events are all keyed on, so the reader cannot invent new
region indices without breaking that binding. Separating two bridged charts is the region
detector's job. The surviving hole — an upper plot with no tick ladder of its own, which
leaves one frame to find and so no competition to detect — is recorded as STATUS item 12.

### White is ink unless the page's own ground says otherwise

The reviewer's ruling on `_paints_nothing` was stricter than "fix the docstring": an opaque
white object on a non-white background IS ink, and one on a white page can erase what was
drawn under it. The round-2 fix already derives the page ground from the page's own drawings
(`_page_ground`: the last opaque fill covering the whole page rect, white when there is
none) and treats a channel as invisible only when it is absent, zero-opacity, or the ground
colour. On a dark page a white rectangle is therefore ink; on a white page a white wrapper
is not. The reviewer's alternative — never exclude on colour at all — would put the SEP
wrappers back and re-merge the five panels, which the competing-frames refusal would then
turn into five refused pages instead of five read ones. Deriving the ground keeps both.

What remains, and is not fixed: a white rectangle drawn OVER earlier content on a white page
erases it, and is still excluded here. That costs a region the reader might otherwise have
been told about; it cannot publish a number.

## Round 3 — the exception, the dead wrapper, and two claims

### The lone-candidate exception, and why deleting it was not enough

`read_bins` waived corroboration when exactly one row passed the span test, on the reasoning
that a lone candidate has nothing to be chosen against. The reviewer showed what else that
branch was holding back. Print one unit word on the label row's OWN baseline, past the end
of the axis — the same `Percent` annotation, moved sideways instead of upward — and the span
test disqualifies the whole label row. The caption below it is then the only candidate, and
the panel published `['Effective', 'federal', 'funds', 'rate']` with a count under
`Effective`, at a residual of 0.0, with no refusal. The round-1 defect, reached through the
exception instead of through proximity. The branch never fired on real pages: the reviewer
instrumented all 198 and reached it 0 times in 835 calls.

Deleting it alone does **not** close that reproducer, which I measured rather than assumed:
with the branch gone, the caption still scores a corroboration of 1, because one bar does
land on one of its words, and it is still the only candidate in the span. The scoring is now
done over EVERY multi-token row below the axis, the span test applied to the WINNER only. The
real label row wins on the bars with 4, fails the span test, and the panel is refused. That
is the honest answer: the page's own best-attested labels are not this frame's.

The span test still decides alone where no bar attests any row — a panel drawn with strays
alone, or a dashed series with no bar resting on the axis. It must then decide uniquely: one
in-span row is the labels, more than one is a choice nothing can settle. Both halves are
load-bearing. Requiring bar corroboration unconditionally refuses the #635 stray-bar panels
(`test_a_bar_owning_no_bin_...`, `test_a_bar_covering_two_bin_centres_...`) and every dashed
staircase, 14 tests from the merged #733; dropping the uniqueness requirement puts the
round-2 caption defect straight back.

### `find_frame` deleted

The wrapper had no caller, and what it did — take the lowest frame and say nothing about the
rest — is the behaviour round 2 exists to remove. Its docstring was a warning to callers it
did not have.

### Two frames: the count is by ladder identity

The refusal fires only where the two plots' tick columns differ in x. Stacked plots drawn in
one column — the SEP layout — put both ladders in one span group, so the region reads as one
frame and the residual gate catches it instead. Both are refusals; the routes differ. STATUS
item 12 now says so, and the reviewer's control is in the suite.

### A negative bound was a void Stage 0 key

`_ATOM_SPLIT_RE` treated a leading U+2212 as a range dash, so `−0.37-−0.13` split into an
empty atom and `_key_atoms` returned `None`: a derived column that could never be matched to
a withheld one, on 4 labels over two ZIRP-era minutes pages. A dash is now a range dash only
where it follows the end of a token; one that opens the key, or follows another dash, is the
sign of the bound it is attached to, and `_WHOLE_TOKEN_RE` admits that sign. All 5 860 bin
labels over the 175 minutes pages and all 1 217 over the SEP pages now parse.

## Round 4 — the fallback needs both halves

The reviewer's round-3 P1 is real and I reproduced it: a dashed staircase, no bar resting on
the axis, one `Percent` printed on the label row's own baseline past the end of the axis. The
span test removes the label row, the prose caption below it is then uniquely in span, and
uniqueness hands it the bins — `('Effective',3) ('federal',3) ('funds',U) ('rate',5)` where
the page draws 3, 5, 4, 2. The counts move because the caption's word centres redefine the
bin intervals. Residual 0.0000, no refusal. Uniqueness was reading its own exclusion as
evidence.

### The proposed remedy publishes the round-1 defect, measured not argued

The review asks for the nearest multi-token row instead of the unique one, and reports that
it "reads correctly" the one repository test it costs. It does not. I applied it and ran the
existing suite: `test_when_no_bar_attests_a_row_the_span_test_must_decide_alone` fails with a
PUBLISHED panel whose bins are `Percent-B2`, `...` — the caption taking the bins and the real
labels absorbed into it as a second atom line, which is the round-1 defect verbatim. The
nearest-row rule assumes the labels are the first thing printed under the axis; on the shape
this reader exists for they are not, because the chart's own x-unit annotation is set between
the axis and its labels. `_aligned` cannot save it either: it accepts the label row as a
second LINE of the caption, which is how the join happens.

So the two candidate rules are each other's counterexample, and neither is safe alone. Where
no bar attests any row the label row must now be **both**: the first multi-token row under
the axis, and the only one of them drawn inside the axis' span. The reviewer's page refuses
because the nearest row is not in span; the round-1 page refuses because the nearest row is
not alone in span. Three difference-pins in `tests/test_gh735_sep_reader.py` carry it — the
stray token alone, the caption alone, and dashed-versus-filled on the same drawing, the last
showing the fallback is only reached where the bars say nothing.

What it costs is the reviewer's own control: a dashed panel with a prose caption BELOW its
labels now refuses, where the nearest-row rule would read it. That is a recall loss rather
than a wrong number, it is the honest answer where nothing on the page can separate the two
rows, and the branch is reached 0 times in 835 calls over the 198 corpus pages. STATUS item
16 records it.

## Round 6 — attesting a row is not being that row

Round 5 left the selection a bare maximum: `attested = max(corroboration)`, winner =
`plural[corroboration.index(attested)]`, with no test that the winning row is a label row
at all. Two reviewers found the same surviving class from three directions, all three with
the bin labels present as ordinary text inside the axis' span, all three built from this
branch's own fixtures with no mock anywhere:

- **(a) the tie.** `bars={'B2': 5}`, an ordinary sparse chart with the `Percent range`
  annotation above the labels. The single bar covers exactly one printed label centre and
  exactly one caption word, both rows score 1, and the tie-break to the upper row published

- **(b) the stray.** `bars={}` with one rectangle at x 235–242 resting on the axis between
  the real label centres. Without a caption round 5 correctly refuses. With it, the
  caption's first word lies over the stray, so the caption scores 1 and the complete
  printed label row scores 0 — the mark no bin can claim becomes valid by choosing the
  prose that covers it.
- **(c) the stood-on caption.** Four narrow bins mid-axis, a four-word caption spaced more
  widely below, and the position of the bars as the only knob. On the label centres:
  `B1..B4` with 3, 5, 4, 2. On the caption's words: `Effective | federal | funds | rate`
  with the same counts, same residual 0.0.

### What was measured before choosing a rule

Every candidate was instrumented over all **840** `read_bins` calls — 103 on the 23 SEP
pages, 732 on the 175 minutes pages, 5 on the reference — before anything was written. The
instrument is committed as `socr-measure-chart-bins`
(`src/socr/figures/measure_chart_bins.py`); it takes the corpus directories as arguments
and prints the counts this section quotes for the SELECTION rule, so those do not
have to be taken on trust. It does not emit every number here: the 44 footnote ties
and the 8 silent-rival pitch figures came from instruments written for the rival rule,
which round 7 deleted.

```
uv run socr-measure-chart-bins <sep-dir> <minutes-dir> <reference-dir>
```

The table below is measured under **round 5's scoring** — a bar corroborates a row when it
covers exactly one of its token centres — because that is the scoring those candidate rules
would have used. Under the containment test this round ships, the same corpus gives 827
calls with two or more corroborated rows rather than 833; the tool prints both.

Four of the five candidates the round-6 brief proposed are refuted by the corpus itself:

| Candidate rule | Corpus verdict |
| :--- | :--- |
| refuse when more than one row is corroborated | 833 of 840 calls have two or more |
| refuse on a tie at the best score | 826 of 840 calls tie — the second label line ties by construction, and a footnote ties on 44 |
| the winner must carry the most tokens of any corroborated row | fails on 74 calls, exactly as the brief warned for two-line labels |
| the winner's token count must equal the number of bars | true on 0 of 103 SEP calls, 12 of 840 overall |
| at least two attesting bars, in distinct bins | 62 calls are attested by exactly one bar |

Three invariants did hold on all 840: every bar that attested the winner covered exactly
one of its centres, the winner was always the **topmost** plural in-span row below the
axis, and no corpus panel prints a plural in-span row *above* its bin labels — which also
corrects a claim this log has carried since round 4. The `Percent range` annotation of the
real SEP and minutes pages is drawn BELOW the label row, not between it and the axis; the
"corpus shape" that defeated round 4's conjunction is a fixture, not a page.

### What shipped

Two admission tests, neither a threshold, each comparing the drawing against itself:

1. **`_attesting_bars`** — a bar attests a row only if, besides covering exactly one of its
   token centres, it lies INSIDE the interval that row's own centres derive for that bin. A
   histogram bar spans its bin and stops where the neighbour starts. Measured: every
   attesting bar on every one of the 840 calls is inside its bin, with at least **1.08pt**
   of clearance, and no winner moves. It closes (a), where a 56pt bar reaches into the
   16pt interval two adjacent caption words derive.
2. **`_unruled_out_rival`** — the winner is discarded when another row below the axis is an
   equally good home for the same marks: a row the bars are SILENT about, carrying at least
   as many columns, whose own tightest column the bars would fit inside. It closes (b) and
   (c), in both of which the printed label row sits there with nothing said about it.

The rival test is asymmetric because the corpus is: since the attested row is the topmost
on all 840 calls, a silent row ABOVE the winner is a rival on a tie, while one BELOW must
carry strictly more columns. Without that, the pages' own prose would refuse everything —
`Percent range`, `Number of participants`, and the footnotes are all drawn below the bins.
Eight minutes panels carry a silent footnote with strictly MORE tokens than their bin row —
11 have one with at least as many, and the 3 that tie are not rivals below. In every one of
the 8, the widest bar (31.5–45.4pt) exceeds the footnote's tightest column (9.0–12.0pt) by a
factor of **2.625 to 5.042**, so the drawing rules it out rather than a cutoff doing it. Two
independent reviewers measured the same 8 and the same ratio range from their own
instruments; "three to four times", written here first, excluded both extremes.

`_aligned` was tightened at the same time: a second line of a two-line label carries exactly
one token per column and no more. That is what let a four-label row be absorbed into a
two-word caption as `Percent-B2 | range-B3`. **826** second lines are absorbed across the
840 calls — 103 SEP, 718 minutes, 5 reference, so 821 over the two corpora without it — and
every one carries exactly one token per column. The figure first published here, 823,
matched no scope and was an arithmetic slip; `socr-measure-chart-bins` now prints it.

## Round 7 — the bins are a row of numbers, and the geometric defence is deleted

Two reviewers took round 6 apart independently, and agreed on where it stood. `_attesting_bars`
— a bar attests a row only if it lies inside the bin that row's own centres derive — survived
both attacks and is KEPT. `_unruled_out_rival` was broken in **both** directions:

- **fabrication through it.** A silent row below the winner must carry strictly more columns
  to be a rival, so four printed bin labels below a four-word caption rule out nothing
  (`test_o`); and because the rule compares the widest bar on the WHOLE axis with the rival's
  tightest column, one extra 25pt mark drawn far from everything, covering no token at all,
  switches the rival test off and hands the caption the bins back (`test_p`);
- **false refusal of a correct chart.** A five-word footnote printed under a chart whose bars
  attest its own labels, over nothing and covered by nothing, made that footnote a silent
  rival and refused the whole panel (`test_q`).

The owner's ruling was to ship best-effort numeric-chart extraction rather than keep narrowing
a geometric exclusion. So round 7 **deletes `_unruled_out_rival` outright** — not patched, not
narrowed — and adds one rule in its place.

### The rule

`_numeric_row`: every token of a candidate row must be a number, or a range of numbers, as
printed. The grammar is Stage 0's own `chart_data._key_atoms`, so the published-key parser and
the bin-row parser cannot drift apart; each atom must then parse as a number, which is the step
that grammar does not take (it accepts `B1` and `Effective` as well-formed keys). A row with
a token no float parse accepts cannot be the bins. The gate is a float parse rather than a
word test, so it admits a numeric PREFIX (`0.13-`, once the trailing range dash is
stripped) and it judges the primary row only, leaving a joined label free to be something
Stage 0's grammar rejects. Where no row below the axis is all-numeric, the panel is
refused.

### The first version of the gate was wrong, and the corpus dumps caught it

Worth recording, because the measurement that cleared it was measuring the wrong objects.
The gate was first written to hand each token of a candidate row straight to `_key_atoms`,
and checked against every **published** bin label — 1 217 SEP, 5 860 minutes, 65 reference,
all of which parse. But a published label is the JOIN of two printed lines, and the row the
gate actually judges is the first of them. Both corpora draw their bins as `0.13-` above
`0.37`, so that row's tokens carry a TRAILING range dash, which `_key_atoms` splits into
`0.13` and an empty part and calls malformed.

The result: the gate rejected the real label row on all 103 SEP calls, fell through to the
second line, and published `0.37` where the page says `0.13-0.37`; on the minutes pages it
moved 5 panels, 49 integers and 5 refusals. Nothing in the test suite saw it. The corpus
dumps did, on the first run. The fix is the one `_join_atoms` already makes for the same
reason — strip a trailing range dash before parsing — and it is pinned directly by
`test_a_two_line_range_label_with_a_trailing_dash_is_still_the_bins` so the corpus is not
the only thing standing between this and a silent relabelling.

### What it cost, measured

Nothing on the corpus, measured on the rows the gate actually judges: on all **840** calls
the attested row is all-numeric (`winner_is_numeric` 840 of 840), **no** call has zero
numeric rows below its axis, and 1 666 rows below an axis are all-numeric across the three
corpora. Both corpus dumps re-run and compared: byte-identical — 108 / 99 / 1 344 / 1 164
and 750 / 635 / 9 971 / 1 033 with 115 refusals. `READER_VERSION` moves `635-stage1/8` →
`635-stage1/9`.

What it does cost is charts whose bins are not numeric. The repository's synthetic fixtures
were relabelled `B1..B5` → `1.0..5.0` for that reason, and the reviewers' probes still drawn
with `B`-labels now refuse **by design rather than by defect** — replayed with numeric labels,
`test_q` reads correctly with and without its footnote (the false refusal is gone with the rule
that caused it), `test_o` reads with the bars on the labels and refuses with them on the
caption, and `test_p` refuses both arms.

### What is still open, and is not hidden

The numeric gate stops all three round-6 constructions from publishing under prose, though
the reviewer is right that "all three refuse" overstates it: two refuse outright, and the
one whose stray mark sits among real numeric labels now READS those labels and leaves the
bins the stray contests UNRESOLVED. That is an improvement rather than a refusal, and it is
worth naming precisely. It does **not** close the class. Astra's numeric counterpart defeats it and was
reproduced against this build: bin labels `1.0 2.0 3.0 4.0` at x = 160…220 with a numeric
annotation `0 5 10 15` above them at x = 130…310, and the bars stood on the annotation. Every
token parses, every bar sits wholly inside its annotation-derived 60pt bin, no bar covers a real
label centre — the annotation scores 4 to 0 and the panel publishes **3, 5, 4, 2 under
`0 | 5 | 10 | 15`**.

One attempt at closing it cheaply was spent and **abandoned**, deliberately rather than
inconclusively: the only invariant left in hand is that the attested row is the topmost in-span
row, and in this construction the fabricating row IS the topmost — the corpus invariant endorses
it. Every other separator available is the bar-width comparison that round 7 exists to delete.
It is pinned as `test_a_numeric_annotation_row_still_takes_the_bins`, which asserts the
defective behaviour so it cannot drift silently, and recorded as STATUS item 18.

The honest summary of what Stage 1 now establishes: a row is **label-shaped** and **attested by
the marks**. Never that it IS the labels. Published chart tables remain UNVERIFIED unless a
caller's `chart_constraint_hook` accepts them.
