Owner rulings

  • 2026-10-08 (#1048): a scanned-page table that could not be checked ships marked "unverified"; a table the check contradicts stays withheld.
  • 2026-10-09: OCR'd text outside a figure is substituted into the prose in place; text inside a figure goes beneath the linked figure image.
  • 2026-10-10 (#1053, #1075): an undecided figure check keeps the page's native text, flags the page (WARNING, FIGURE_WORDS_UNREAD) and escalates the figure crop, never the page.
  • 2026-10-10 (#1078): the formula lane is judged on correct maths first, GPU time second.
  • 2026-10-10 (#1080, #1081): every run checks the tools its enabled features need before reading a page, and stops when one is missing. A plain check, not a model.
  • 2026-10-11 (#1082): on a scanned table, the same model may confirm its own number from a one-cell crop (default off). Built, then parked.
  • Standing: digit disagreements never go to the text-only judge (Jev); they are settled mechanically or by a vision read.

Open decisions for the owner, in order

  1. Scanned tables that disagree with Tesseract. Today they are withheld. On the 12 hand-checked pages, shipping the model's tables would have shipped 11 correct pages and one (Forney p22) with two wrong numbers. A. Keep withholding: about 160 pages stay dropped, no wrong number ships through this path. B. Ship with a visible "unchecked" mark: about 160 pages return, roughly 1 in 12 carries a wrong number under the mark. No mechanical test found so far separates a Tesseract misread from a model error on these pages.
  2. May a checked formula reading replace the native text? The September equation-lane ruling says model readings are attached beside the native text, never replace it. The formula check proves "nothing invented or dropped", not sub/superscript placement (x_t^2 and x^t_2 both pass).
  3. A formula the lane cannot read: keep the page's native text with a warning (as figures do), or send the whole page to the model (today).

Evidence so far

  • Formulas, today: the re-route for damaged-maths text layers (#960) fired 1,207 times on the reference run; of 1,655 maths-flagged pages the model read whole, 1,203 were flagged by the math-font signal alone. A hand check of 20 such pages found 9 correct (false alarm), 8 flattened and 3 with junk characters. A layout probe flagged 30 of 66 sampled native pages as flattened, about 24 really so.
  • Formulas, record-only lane (branch impl/exp-formula-shadow, not merged): third round, 20 passing readings checked by eye, 16 fully correct. Reading a page's formulas took about 45 s on the Mac against 18.8 s (median) for one whole-page read on the cluster: the lane is about correctness, not time.
  • Tables on scanned pages: 181 pages in 74 papers were dropped because the cluster had no Tesseract. With it, 19 of the 181 pass (17 after #1085). Of 92 disputed numbers on 12 random failing pages, the model was right on 90; Tesseract dropped signs and decimal points, missed cells and misread digits. 11 of the 12 pages had a fully correct table. The one real error (Forney 1988 p22) is a row the model shifted next to blank cells.
  • Figures: figures out of reading order (93 pages), side-by-side panels (55), drawn vector charts, crops merged with neighbours (#1076) and one real figure lost by the #1069 rule (Bianchi p58) are open.
  • Text: invisible text layers over a scan (226 pages) and control bytes before digits (152 pages) also trigger whole-page reads. No check on native text exists yet (#1050, #1051).
  • Prior constraint: docs/plans/fake-native-pages closed a lexical text-quality signal unbuilt as noise; Jev's garble role in #1051 must beat that or be dropped.

How socr routes a page today

Drawn from origin/main @ c4937472 (2026-10-09): core/born_digital.py (classify), pipeline/orchestrator.py (lanes), pipeline/agentic.py + core/providers.py (ladder). Since then figure pages get one crop per figure (#1075) and every run starts with a tool check (#1081); the lanes are otherwise as drawn.

The three orange spots

  1. Local is priced at zero. The ladder sorts by cost_per_page_usd, and local qwen is 0.0. So the router has no reason to avoid it: GPU minutes and long-input hallucination never enter the decision. Your point 1 is invisible to the cost model.
  2. Trusted native is unchecked. The cheapest lane is also the one nothing looks at. Skipping more OCR makes this lane bigger, so the miss rate has to be measured (#1050) before we widen it.
  3. Whole page or nothing. A semi-scanned page with one bad paragraph sends the entire page to the model: the longest input, the highest hallucination risk, and it overwrites the good native prose too. Tables and math already work by crops; body text does not.

The routing we want

Blue = new or changed. Purple = Jev (text-only judge). Grey already exists. Text quality check: #1050 (missing text), #1051 (wrong text). Figure crops: #1053.

What changes

  1. Four content lanes, each with its own check. Text, formulas, figures, tables. A whole page goes to a model only when the text layer is unusable.
  2. Text quality check, for free. Step 1, missing text: ink with no text-layer words on it (#1050). Step 2, wrong text: a Tesseract re-read compared with the native text; matching lines pass, differing words go to Jev, any differing digit goes to the vision model (#1051). Failures are sent to the model as crops and spliced back in place.
  3. Figure quality check (new). Each figure is cropped on its own (#1053), its words read by the model, then checked against a Tesseract read of the same crop, and every "Figure N" caption must have a crop. Figure boxes also tell step 1 which ink is a figure mark rather than missing text.
  4. Formulas are the weak spot. LaTeX validation checks that a formula parses, not that it matches the page. No fix is proposed yet.
  5. Fail closed. A check that cannot run or cannot decide sends the page to the whole-page route. Figures are the exception (ruled 2026-10-10): keep the page's text, flag the page, send only the figure's crop on.
  6. Honest local price. GPU seconds enter the ladder's cost.
  7. Jev (purple) judges text, never pixels. It classifies text blocks, judges word differences, and picks crop vs whole page. Digit disagreements never go through it.
